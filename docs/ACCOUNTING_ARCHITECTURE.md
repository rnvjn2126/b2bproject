# Accounting architecture

A separate Django app references the existing company workspace. Account codes, group codes and posting keys are workspace scoped. An Indian starter chart is installed on demand and can be extended. The first UI uses an April–March financial year with monthly periods. The posting interface uses accounting dates in India time. Dimensions are workspace-specific branches and cost centres.

Money is accepted as Decimal with at most two fractional digits. Each line is debit or credit, every posted journal has at least two lines and its debit/credit totals agree. The posting service inserts the journal and lines and seals them within one transaction. Posting and period lock operations serialize on the same workspace row. Only sealed records appear in journal, audit and ledger reports. A stable `JRN-<id>` reference is guaranteed unique by the journal primary key. External references remain descriptive. The unique submission key prevents duplicate posting, and replaying the same key with different data is rejected.

Opening balances use one balanced opening journal per workspace. Period reports derive opening, movements and closing amounts from posted lines. Adjustments are separate journals. Corrections reverse the original lines in a new journal in an open period. Only one reversal of an original is allowed. The initial scope uses INR and a single branch and cost centre per journal. Branch-by-branch opening imports and year-end retained earnings carry-forward remain separate work. Period locks are one-way in the initial UI.

Receipt and payment vouchers move an explicitly selected amount into or out of the starter cash or bank ledgers. Contra vouchers transfer between those two ledgers. Voucher types are validated in the posting service as well as the form. A payment reference is descriptive. These vouchers do not clear invoices or verify a bank transaction.

Voucher review adds internal draft numbering (`VCH-<id>`), review and owner approval. Draft versions reject stale edits and approval of a changed allocation. Approval creates and links one sealed journal in the same transaction. In the initial internal posting conventions, debit notes reduce a supplier liability and credit notes reduce a customer asset balance. Output and input tax, statutory note formats, customer and supplier subledger allocations and configurable invoice series belong to later checkpoints. Companies can rerun financial year setup to add the expanded groups. Existing ledger assignments are retained.

Financial statements use posted lines. Profit and loss reports activity within the selected date range. The balance sheet includes all entries through the end date, plus accumulated net activity still recorded in income and expense accounts. Branch filters include only journals explicitly assigned to that branch. These are ledger summaries. Inventory costing, gross and operating profit classifications, period closing and statutory presentation remain separate work.

The `orders` app uses one sales order per quotation series and snapshots accepted customer and selling data. Managers with active seats and company owners can create and approve orders. One purchase order covers one requirement and offer, with separate purchase orders for split suppliers. Purchases snapshot normalized material rates before tax and freight. Existing quote margin protections are rechecked before conversion. Customer and supplier order PDFs use separate fields.

Receipts and dispatches record confirmed quantities in the sales requirement unit. Dispatch is limited to receipts through its date. Procurement and dispatch share a company lock and reference and quantity checks. Cancellation releases unreceived purchase allocations and is blocked after movement or while dependent orders remain open. These movements do not change stock valuation or post revenue. Inventory-backed dispatch and costing are later work.

Issue an invoice and its journal within one database transaction, with a unique source link and immutable copies of prices, items and party details. Allocate quantities to deliveries and invoices under locks so replays and concurrent requests cannot bill the same quantity twice. The statutory invoice design and the workflow for confirming the posting event still need professional review.
